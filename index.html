Welcome aboard!

Inside Earth, hot mantle rock rises, spreads below the lithosphere, cools, and sinks. This slow convection helps move tectonic plates. At plate boundaries, those movements build ridges, trenches, volcanoes, and mountain ranges.

Mid-Ocean Ridge

Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Iceland

Iceland is part of the divergent Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where the North American and Eurasian plates pull apart. Convection-driven movement lets hot mantle rise. As pressure drops, the mantle partially melts to make magma. The magma rises through cracks and solidifies into new basaltic ocean crust. Repeated spreading widens the Atlantic and pushes the ridge upward, creating the submarine chain and the visible rift landscape in Iceland.

Iceland — Mid-Atlantic Ridge

Trench

Mariana Trench, Pacific Ocean

The Mariana Trench marks a convergent, ocean-ocean plate boundary. The denser Pacific Plate bends and sinks beneath the smaller Mariana Plate in a process called subduction. As the descending plate drags the seafloor downward, it creates a deep V-shaped trench. The trench is the deepest known ocean floor, while water and sediment released from the slab help produce volcanic activity farther west.

Mariana Trench — western Pacific Ocean

Volcano

Kīlauea, Hawaiʻi

Kīlauea formed above the Hawaiʻi hot spot, a persistent area of unusually hot mantle. Rising mantle rock partially melts and produces magma. The magma moves upward through cracks in the Pacific Plate and erupts at the surface. As the Pacific Plate slowly moves northwest over the stationary hot spot, repeated eruptions have built a chain of Hawaiian islands. Kīlauea is a shield volcano with broad slopes and fluid basaltic lava.

Kīlauea — Hawaiʻi Island

Mountain

The Himalayas and Mount Everest

Mount Everest and the Himalayas formed where the Indian and Eurasian plates converge. Neither continental plate easily subducts because both are relatively light. Instead, strong compression pushed and folded the crust, while thrust faults stacked rock layers upward. Mantle convection continues to help drive plate movement, but uplift and erosion shape the spectacular elevation of this collision mountain range.

Mount Everest — Himalayan mountain range
